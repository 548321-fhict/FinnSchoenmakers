Project Splitstar

Overview

Splitstar is a combat-focused 2.5D shooter where players progress through a series of rooms, fighting enemies and mastering movement mechanics. I worked on the level design, visual effects, and user interface.

My goal was to create a clear, engaging experience with carefully paced encounters and a gradual increase in difficulty. Each room introduces a new challenge while reinforcing the game's core mechanics, giving the level a sense of progression.

Features

Spatial Design & Player Flow

I divided the level into rooms, each with a clear gameplay purpose. The layout, enemy placement, and environment guide players without explicit instructions, so they can focus on the encounter rather than searching for where to go.

Room size and shape help control the pace. Smaller spaces create pressure and limit movement, while larger arenas reward positioning and awareness. The route stays clear, but each encounter challenges players to decide how to move through it.

Room Design

Each room has a distinct role in the progression, building from the introduction of the core mechanics to a final test of the player's skills.

  • Introduction: teaches the core mechanics and basic combat.
  • Vertical gameplay: focuses on upward movement and avoiding hazards.
  • Transition space: combines movement styles to create a smoother flow.
  • Gauntlet: tests endurance and punishes mistakes.
  • Boss arena: uses an open layout that demands constant movement and awareness.

Environmental Design & Readability

I designed the environment to make gameplay clear while maintaining a consistent visual style. Walls, floors, and obstacles define movement space and interaction areas. Careful spacing and alignment help prevent visual clutter during combat.

Curved wall elements make the rooms feel less artificial. I also enclosed the level to maintain immersion and hide the world beyond the playable area.

Weapon Feedback

I designed the weapon effects to communicate player actions and make combat feel responsive. Muzzle flashes provide immediate feedback when firing, while impact sparks make hits easier to read and more satisfying.

I kept the bullet visuals in a separate system to avoid workflow conflicts while still representing the intended behavior. This let me develop the feedback effects without interfering with other parts of the workflow.

Explosion Effect

The explosion was the most complex effect to create because I couldn't find suitable references that matched the game's style. I decided to build it in layers, starting with sparks adapted from the impact effect and a spherical smoke burst based on a modified dust effect.

I created the fire by adjusting the smoke system, then added a flash of light to increase the effect's intensity. Together, these elements create a cohesive explosion that fits the game's visual style.

HUD Structure

I divided the HUD into two parts to keep information organized. The player-focused section displays health, ability cooldowns, and active power-ups. These details stay readable at a glance so players can make quick decisions during combat.

The system-focused section shows progression details, such as the current room and score. This gives players useful context without distracting them from the action.

Development

Process

I started by mapping out the level in Figma, then built greybox blockouts in Unreal Engine to test navigation, scale, and movement. This gave me a chance to check the level's basic flow before committing to detailed environments.

During playtests, I checked whether the layout felt clear and intuitive. After receiving feedback, I refined the level by adding elevation, increasing the challenge in key areas, and introducing paths that asked players to weigh risk against reward.

Key Learnings

Structured pacing and difficulty progression sustain engagement. Varied rooms and encounters can challenge players without overwhelming them.

Greyboxing supports fast iteration. Testing navigation and scale early helps prevent major redesigns later.

Player feedback is essential. Playtesting helped validate my design decisions and gave me confidence to move on to final polish.

VFX has more impact when integrated early. Adding effects late in production limits how much they can contribute to the overall experience.

Clear UI improves readability. Functional clarity can support gameplay without adding visual complexity.
